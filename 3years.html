3 years in software

abdulrahman tabaza, 28/8/2026

[home]

i've been working in machine learning and software for 3 years, my benchmark for progress is deeply personal and subjective, and frankly quite selfish, but i stand by it, so i'd thought i'd write about it.

2023

my projects resided in notebooks, i was lucky enough to work on new systems, pms pitching a pain point, crux of llm boom, my team pitched llm solutions, and the projects moved forward, but the code never left a python notebook save for the last week we were told it's going to production. i didn't have an app state, no dbs whatsoever, no docker images, just notebook export to .py and run with it, also i wasn't taking ownership of my work when it was about to be deployed.

2024

new year new me, byebye notebooks and colab and kaggle, arguably one of the worst things to be taught to "ai students" is managed one click environments like jupyter, they're easy and convenient and free but severely limiting to your development as a software engineer, so if you want to shoot yourself in the foot, don't try to break colab and run a reverse proxy on it to access it's gpu while trying to understand the kaggle docker image, stay in the notebook, but i left. i turned to information retrieval papers and solr and colbert and splade and learning to rank, and i ran wild, at the cost of one gigantic main.py file with 2000 lines of code for a fastapi file, still no app state, no database, but now, a script, a 14 gb virtual environment even though i ported models to onnx manually from mteb because flash attention is very fun, and a dockerfile at the root of the repo.

2025

old and withered, you turn to ancient software, portability was always the name of the game, 4kb of vram and good luck writing a shader for that, yet they did, and here i am, with exposure to over 30 years of hardware and software optimization, and i'm growing, i write fastapi apps, i write docker images, i used gguf and onnx quants, but alas, my docker image is still 14 gbs (thank you torch vision nms, you vile beast), and sadly, no app state, no database, no testing, no auth, even docker compose was still foreign to me, i couldn't get two networks to talk to each other. and for a fun game: take a wild guess if any line of code i wrote in 2023 & 2024 was ever tested.

2026

the grass is green, the air is crisp, the slop is impending, lies and noise and cess pools brew, fanning out vile detritus as it passes, but we must persevere, there is a tiny slither of hope for software and systems. alas, my docker image is 300 mbs, my endpoints are authenticated, my state is in a database, my models don't need a python cuda runtime or the python cuda runtime is exiled in its own plane of existence like the bloated leper it is (goddamn it i hate cuda), services and hardware are observable, code is tested, my complex systems are torn down in simulators with faults injected, my deployment and testing is in a ci pipeline, i'm deploying my own work.

i've often championed for source control for personal projects just as artificats of darker time, i have the grotesque streamlit demos i wrote in uni so i can look at them and gauge if i'm moving forward or not, and i am, so i'll be safe, head down, learn all you can, your skillset will get you work, cesspools die down eventually, noise gets tamed, and the lepers and charlatans will get ousted and exiled, and the water will run clean of all the excrement, we'll just design and write systems that do something, and sell them, one step at a time.
